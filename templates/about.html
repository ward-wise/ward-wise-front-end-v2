Ward Wise

A clearer view of every ward.

Ward Wise turns Chicago's open data into measures of neighborhood wellbeing that anyone can explore, weight and compare. Volunteers build it at Chi Hack Night.

Built from public records

Every measure comes from an agency or open dataset, and the metric dictionary names the source for each one.

Shaped by residents

Many measures started as resident nominations, from parking difficulty to lead in park water fountains.

The same for every ward

One method covers all 50 wards and 77 community areas, so any two places can be compared fairly.

Open to everyone

The site is free with no login, and the data is available through a public API.

Our goal

A measure nominated from every ward.

The best measures come from the people who live with them. Nominate one, and your ward gets the credit on the map.

Where it started · 2024

First, the menu money.

Ward Wise began as a Chi Hack Night breakout group built around participatory budgeting. Every year each alder gets $1.5 million in "menu money" for neighborhood infrastructure such as sidewalks, street resurfacing and bike lanes. The first Ward Wise showed how every ward spent it, and how residents could have a say.

Sean MacMullan presented that version at Chi Hack Night on May 28, 2024. Menu money is still part of the site, and participatory budgeting is one of the measures in the catalog.

Since May 2026

Refreshed, and still growing.

In May 2026 the team expanded Ward Wise to help citizens explore Chicago data more broadly. The refreshed dashboard opened with 20 measures for the Bean's 20th birthday and has added more every month since.
