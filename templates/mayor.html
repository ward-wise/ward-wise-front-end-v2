How this works

Two lines, twenty-four statements, one source per fact. Side to side is the economy: progressive (more public programs, more taxes on business and wealth) at one end, conservative (lower taxes, less spending) at the other. Nearly every candidate is a Democrat, so conservative here means conservative for Chicago. Up and down is city control: more rules and enforcement up, more personal freedom down. It covers police, but also zoning, permits, speed cameras, short-term rentals and homeless camps.

Ward Wise does not endorse candidates. A dot with fewer than 4 facts on a line is pulled toward the middle and drawn faded, so one strong quote cannot push someone to the edge. Words follow one rule: under 1.5 of 10 from the middle reads "near the middle", 1.5 up to 5 reads "somewhat toward", and 5 or more reads "strongly toward". Your answers stay in your browser until you finish; then they are counted, without your name or email, to show how Chicago answered.

This page is for learning about the race and may contain errors. A match shows how close your answers are on these two lines only, so a candidate far from you is not wrong for being there. If you spot a mistake, or you are a candidate and want to correct your record, email connor@wardwise.org.

Sources: campaign sites, Chicago Board of Election Commissioners, City Clerk records, news reports, Chicago Public Library. Checked Nov 10, 2026.
