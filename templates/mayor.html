How this works

Two lines, twenty-four statements, one source per fact. Side to side is the economy: progressive (more public programs, more taxes on business and wealth) at one end, conservative (lower taxes, less spending) at the other. Nearly every candidate is a Democrat, so conservative here means conservative for Chicago. Up and down is enforcement: tougher policing and enforcement up, a lighter touch down. It covers police hiring, surveillance, speed cameras, homeless camps and work with federal agents. Five statements on building rules count in your match but do not move your dot.

Ward Wise does not endorse candidates. A dot with fewer than 4 facts on a line is pulled toward the middle and drawn faded, so one strong quote cannot push someone to the edge. A person needs 8 or more sourced answers to be ranked as a match. A candidate with fewer answers can still be placed by leaning: a hollow dot that comes from their own sourced positions on related issues, with each quote checked against its page. A leaning never counts in a match. Words follow one rule: under 1.5 of 10 from the middle reads "near the middle", 1.5 up to 5 reads "somewhat toward", and 5 or more reads "strongly toward". Your answers stay in your browser until you finish; then they are counted, without your name or email, to show how Chicago answered.

This page is for learning about the race and may contain errors. A match counts how close your answers are to a person's sourced answers on these 24 statements, and the map shows these two lines only, so a candidate far from you is not wrong for being there. If you spot a mistake, or you are a candidate and want to correct your record, email connor@wardwise.org.
