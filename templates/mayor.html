How this works

  • You answer 24 statements, and your answers put you on a map with two lines, the economy (progressive to conservative) and enforcement (lighter touch to tougher).
  • Each candidate's dot comes from their own votes, words and campaign pages. Click any dot to see every fact and its source.
  • The candidate whose dot sits closest to yours is your best match.
  • A hollow dot means the candidate answered few of the statements, so their dot comes from their stated positions on related issues.
  • Ward Wise does not endorse candidates.

This page may contain errors. A match compares you on these two lines only, so a candidate far from you is not wrong for being there. To fix a mistake, or if you are a candidate correcting your record, email connor@wardwise.org.

Full method and fact-check history

A dot sits at the average of a person's sourced positions on each line, and a line needs at least 2 sourced facts before it places anyone. The same goes for you: 2 agree or disagree answers on a line. Neutral counts like Not sure. The five housing and building statements are for comparison only and do not move any dot. The percent is closeness on the map, not agreement on every issue, and when the top two are within 3 points the result says it is too close to call. The middle of the economy line is zero. Scored on the same statements, the halfway point between the 2024 national Democratic platform and the 2020 Illinois Republican platform sits within 1 point of zero. Nearly every candidate is a Democrat, so conservative here means conservative for Chicago. Your answers stay in your browser until you finish. Then they are counted, without your name or email, to show how Chicago answered.
